Method and data notes

How the dataset is prepared, how the model works and what the results do and do not show.

Pipeline
  1. 01Prepare data
    Load the household time series, check units and timestamps, handle missing readings and select a consistent group of houses.
  2. 02Standardise intervals
    Use hourly readings. For finer data, confirm what each reading represents before aggregating.
  3. 03Calculate area consumption
    Sum the selected houses at each timestamp to form the Area X Total series.
  4. 04Detect anomalies
    Compare the area total with historical patterns using an unsupervised detector.
  5. 05Identify contributors
    Estimate each house's expected use from its own history, then rank the largest positive deviations.
  6. 06Report results
    Show the anomaly timestamp, actual and expected totals, the increase and the leading houses.

Unit handling
Interval energy (kWh or Wh)Sum across the interval. Confirm each value covers the preceding interval.
Cumulative meter readingTake differences between consecutive readings. Handle resets and negative differences.
Instantaneous power (kW)Do not sum as energy. Convert using the sampling interval.
Limitations.
  • Area X is a constructed study group and does not represent a real neighbourhood, transformer area or feeder.
  • Household excess is a statistical attribution from observed readings and does not prove the cause of an anomaly.
  • The model does not claim to detect theft or faulty equipment.
  • Power cuts appear as zero readings and are handled separately from anomalies.
