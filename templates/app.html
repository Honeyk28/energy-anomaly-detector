Method and data notes

How the dataset is prepared, how the model works and what the results do and do not show.

Pipeline
  1. 01 Prepare data
    Load household time series, verify daily units (t_kWh) and dates, exclude zero readings from totals/baselines, and establish the 38-meter study group.
  2. 02 Standardise intervals
    Daily resolution. Ensure daily energy is consumed that day (not cumulative). Zero readings are flagged as non-reporting days.
  3. 03 Calculate area consumption & baseline
    Calculate house expected from median historical patterns. For each date, sum only comparable houses (both reading & valid expected) to obtain actual and expected area totals.
  4. 04 Detect anomalies
    Evaluate relative percentage increase and absolute increase via unsupervised models (Isolation Forest or Robust z-score MAD). Flag unusually HIGH spikes only.
  5. 05 Identify contributors
    Compute excess = actual minus expected for each comparable house. Rank descending and calculate percentage share of total excess.
  6. 06 Report results
    Present the date, actual vs expected totals, increase kWh, and the leading households driving the excess.

Unit handling
Daily energy (t_kWh) Energy consumed that day in kWh. Zero readings represent outages/missing days and are excluded from baselines.
Cumulative meter reading Take differences between consecutive readings. Handle resets and negative differences.
Instantaneous power (kW) Do not sum as energy. Convert using the sampling interval.
Limitations.
  • Area X has only the houses in the file (fewer than 50 meters, specifically 38 meters) and does not represent a full utility feeder or real neighborhood.
  • Daily resolution only (no intra-day or hourly detection).
  • Many missing days per house (each meter has only ~195 to 624 days of data). Raw daily sums must never be compared across days without comparable filtering.
  • Zero readings are treated as non-reporting or possible power outage days, not genuine zero consumption.
  • No ground-truth labels exist, so accuracy metrics cannot be computed.
  • Household excess is a statistical attribution from observed readings and does not prove the cause of an anomaly. The model does not claim to detect theft or faulty equipment.
